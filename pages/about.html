My story

It started with a Harry Potter forum.

I built my first online community in 1999. I was nine years old, the internet was still dial-up, and nobody called it "community strategy." I started a Harry Potter forum, and it grew far beyond anything I imagined — people from around the world, real friendships, and the chance to share my faith with thousands of people online. I didn't have a framework or a playbook. I just knew people needed connection, and I was stubborn enough to figure out how to create it.

That instinct never left. I went on to manage web, email, and social for one of Nashville's largest multi-campus churches. That job sparked the idea for an online community for church communicators, which I co-founded in 2015. It started as a Facebook group, took on a life of its own, and grew to more than 35,000 church leaders before it was acquired in early 2024.

Along the way I led faith partnerships globally and community partnerships across North America at a Fortune 500 tech company, managed a 700,000-member creative community on Discord, delivered more than 100 trainings, and taught marketing, social media, analytics, and AI at five universities. I wrote the textbook on church communications, and I still teach from inside the church world rather than looking in from outside it.

Here's what I've learned: most people aren't struggling because their message is bad. They're struggling because nobody built the plan for it to spread. The right community, built with intention, changes everything. That's why my work comes down to one idea: automate the temporary, invest in the eternal. Let the tools handle the busywork so you can spend your time on people.
