Let me say something clearly upfront: AI cannot pray. It cannot feel the weight of a congregation that is grieving. It cannot sense the Spirit moving in a room, or know intuitively that a congregation needs gentleness instead of challenge on a particular Sunday. Those things belong entirely to you.

But AI can do a lot of the groundwork that used to take hours — and freeing up that time is, in itself, a gift to your ministry.

## Scripture Study and Theological Research

One of the most immediately practical uses of AI for pastors and church communicators is research. Ask it questions you'd otherwise spend an hour Googling or cross-referencing commentaries for:

- "Summarize how the early church fathers interpreted the feeding of the 5,000."
- "What is the historical context of Paul's letter to the Philippians? What was happening in Philippi at the time?"
- "Compare the Reformed and Arminian interpretations of Romans 8:28–30."
- "What Hebrew word is used for 'peace' in Isaiah 26:3, and what are its nuances beyond the English translation?"

AI is not a replacement for your Bible software, your commentaries, or your own study. But it's a remarkably fast way to get oriented on a topic, surface angles you hadn't considered, and find starting points for deeper research.

## Sermon Brainstorming

Ask AI to help you brainstorm — not to write your sermon, but to help you think. Try prompts like:

- "Give me ten possible entry points for a sermon on grief from John 11."
- "What are five contemporary analogies for the concept of atonement that would resonate with a secular audience?"
- "What common objections do people have to the idea of a good God allowing suffering? List them and summarize common theological responses."

You're not outsourcing your theology. You're expanding the surface area of your thinking before you sit down to do the real work.

## Illustrations and Applications

The hardest part of sermon prep for many preachers isn't the exegesis — it's finding illustrations that land with real people. AI is surprisingly good at generating options:

"Give me five modern-day illustrations of prodigal-son dynamics — situations where someone squanders something, hits rock bottom, and returns to where they started. Include both secular and relatable scenarios."

You'll get a list to work from. The personal story, the pastoral application, the Spirit-led moment — those are still entirely yours.

## One Caution

Always verify AI's theological claims. It can make mistakes — citing things incorrectly, misrepresenting a theologian's position, or confidently stating something that isn't quite right. Treat it like a well-read research assistant, not an authority. Check the important stuff.

AI does the research. You do the discernment. That's the right division of labor.
