The word "advertising" makes some church leaders uncomfortable. It feels too commercial, too corporate. But here's the thing: if you want people to know about your church, your events, or the hope you offer — advertising is simply paying to make sure that message gets in front of the right people.

That's not selling out. That's stewardship.

## Marketing vs. Advertising: What's the Difference?

People often confuse these two. Marketing is the overall strategy — all the ways you communicate who you are and what you're about. Advertising is the specific act of paying to place a message in front of an audience. It's the final P in the classic marketing mix: Promotion.

Your organic social posts are marketing. A Facebook Ad you pay to run is advertising. Both matter. But advertising extends your reach far beyond your existing audience — to people who have never heard of you.

## The 7-Step Church Advertising Plan

1. **Define your target audience.** Who are you trying to reach? Age, location, life stage, interests — the more specific, the better.
2. **Set a goal.** Do you want to inform (new church plant), persuade (come to our Easter service), or remind (we have a grief group that meets on Thursdays)?
3. **Set a budget.** Start small — even $5/day on Facebook Ads can have meaningful impact if targeted well. Look at what you've done before, and decide what you can actually sustain.
4. **Create your content.** Tell a story. Show real people, not just a building. Make it clear what you want the viewer to do next.
5. **Choose your medium.** For most churches starting out, Facebook Ads are the easiest entry point. They have free learning material (Facebook Blueprint) and a relatively intuitive interface.
6. **Choose your timing.** When is your audience most likely to act? Running Easter ads in March is better than the week before Easter.
7. **Track and adjust.** Facebook Ads Manager shows you impressions, clicks, and conversions. If an ad isn't working, fix it or stop it.

## What Should You Advertise?

Not everything needs an ad budget, but these are usually worth it:

- Easter and Christmas services
- Community events and outreach programs
- Recovery ministries (Celebrate Recovery, grief groups, divorce care)
- A new church plant or campus launch
- A sermon series you're particularly proud of

## Free Advertising Worth Doing First

Before you spend a dollar, make sure you've maximized the free options:

- Update your Google Business Profile with accurate hours, photos, and a response to reviews
- Submit events to local online community calendars and newspapers
- Write blog posts answering questions your community is already searching for (this is free SEO)
- Connect with local bloggers, community Facebook Groups, and Nextdoor

The goal of advertising is simple: help people find the hope your church offers. That's worth investing in.
