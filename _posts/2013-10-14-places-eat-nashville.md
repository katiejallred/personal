## A note before you go

I made this list in my first fall as a Nashvillian, back in 2013. Nashville has grown a lot since then, and restaurants change. Some move, some change owners or menus, and some close. So before you drive across town for anything on this list, check the restaurant's website, social media, or map listing for current hours and location. Treat this as a list of ideas, not a guarantee.

## Nashville neighborhoods worth exploring

The best way to eat your way around Nashville is by neighborhood. Park once, walk around, and try a few places.

- **12South.** A walkable stretch south of downtown with shops, coffee, and dessert.
- **East Nashville.** Across the river from downtown, with a local, neighborhood feel and plenty of restaurants and coffee shops.
- **Germantown.** Just north of downtown, near the Nashville Farmers' Market and Bicentennial Capitol Mall State Park.
- **The Gulch and Midtown.** Newer, busier, and good for a night out.
- **Downtown and Lower Broadway.** Honky-tonks, live music, and crowds. Fun to see at least once.
- **Hillsboro Village.** Near Vanderbilt and Belmont, with a college-town feel.
- **Just outside the city.** Towns like Franklin and Nolensville have their own main streets and local spots, and they are worth the short drive.

## What to eat in Nashville

If it's your first visit, here's what I'd try:

- **Hot chicken.** Nashville's signature dish: fried chicken coated in a spicy, peppery paste. Prince's is often credited with starting the tradition. Spice levels vary a lot from place to place, so start lower than you think.
- **Meat-and-three.** A Southern classic. Pick one meat and three sides, usually from a daily menu. Save room for the cornbread.
- **Biscuits and Southern breakfast.** Nashville takes breakfast seriously, and weekend brunch lines prove it.
- **Barbecue.** Plenty of options around town, many with their own take on sauce and sides.
- **Coffee and dessert.** Local coffee shops and ice cream stops are part of the fun. (Start with my [Jeni's Ice Cream review](/jenis-ice-cream-review/).)

## Tips for visiting Nashville restaurants

- **Check hours before you go.** Many local spots close one day a week or only serve breakfast and lunch.
- **Make a reservation for dinner,** especially on weekends and downtown.
- **Expect lines at the famous places.** Go early, go on a weekday, or put your name in and take a walk.
- **Plan for parking.** Downtown and the busiest neighborhoods usually mean paid parking, so rideshare can be easier.
- **Follow food trucks online.** Their locations change day to day, so check their social media for schedules.
- **Ask a local.** Your server, barista, or hotel front desk will usually have a favorite, and it's often somewhere you'd never find on your own.

Want to pair dinner with a show? See my photos from [Opry Country Classics at the Ryman](/opry-country-classics-at-the-ryman/). However you plan your trip, come hungry. Nashville is a great food town.
