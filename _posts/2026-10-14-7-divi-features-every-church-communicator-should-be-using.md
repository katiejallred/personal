Divi has features built for exactly the kind of work you do every week: repeating content, rotating volunteers, and never enough time. Here are seven I think every church communicator should be using. There's a scorecard at the bottom. See how many you can tick.

## 1. Theme Builder templates

If you're designing every sermon post or event page from scratch, stop. Divi's Theme Builder lets you design a template once and apply it to every post of that type.

Build one template for sermons, one for events, and one for staff bios. From then on, adding a new sermon means filling in a title, a video link, and a date. The layout takes care of itself. This one feature alone can give you back an hour a week.

## 2. Design variables

Your church has a brand whether you like it or not. The question is whether your website shows it consistently.

Divi 5's design variables let you store your colors, fonts, and sizes in one place and use them everywhere. Change your primary blue next year? Change it once, and it updates across the whole site.

## 3. Presets

Variables handle single values. Presets save a module's whole look: your button style, your heading style, your card style. Style it once, save it as a preset, and every new button you add looks right.

{% include screenshot.html src="/assets/uploads/2026/divi/builder-presets.jpg" alt="Divi 5's Preset Manager listing module types such as Accordion, Audio, Blog, Blurb, and Button" caption="The Preset Manager. Every module type can have saved styles your whole team reuses." %}

Decide once, and you never have to decide again.

## 4. The Divi Library

Every church website has sections that show up again and again: the "Plan a Visit" call to action, the service times block, the giving button.

Save those sections to the Divi Library as global items. Drop them onto any page. When your service times change for summer, update the global item once and every page updates with it.

## 5. The Role Editor

Here's the scenario: a well-meaning volunteer is updating the youth page and accidentally deletes your header. It happens to all of us.

Divi's Role Editor lets you decide what each WordPress user role can do in the builder. Let volunteers edit text and images while locking layouts and styles. Support them. Trust them. And protect them from breaking things.

## 6. Responsive editing

Most people visiting your church website are on their phone. Divi lets you adjust font sizes, spacing, and even hide or show sections separately for desktop, tablet, and phone. Click the phone icon at the top of the builder to switch.

{% include screenshot.html src="/assets/uploads/2026/divi/builder-phone-view.jpg" alt="The Divi builder showing a page at phone width" caption="Phone view. Fix the awkward heading wrap before a first-time guest ever sees it." %}

Once a month, open your site on your phone and look at it the way a first-time guest would. If a heading wraps awkwardly or a button is too small, fix it right there in the phone view.
