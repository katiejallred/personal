Let's build a sermon archive and an events page you'll actually keep updated, because updating them will take five minutes instead of an hour.

## Why this matters more than you think

Your sermon archive isn't just for members who missed a Sunday. It's often the first place a curious guest goes to answer, "What does this church actually teach?" They want to hear your pastor before they ever sit in a pew.

And your events page answers the next question: "Is there a way for someone like me to get connected?" If your events page is out of date, the answer looks like no.

## Step 1: Set up the structure (once)

Before you design anything, set up WordPress so every sermon has a home.

- **Create a Sermons category.** Every sermon becomes a post in that category.
- **Use tags for series and books of the Bible.** Tag each sermon with its series name and scripture book, so people can browse "Romans" or "Advent 2026."
- **Decide what every sermon includes.** I recommend a title, the speaker, the scripture, the date, a video, and a two-sentence summary. That's it.

## Step 2: Design one sermon template with the Theme Builder

Now open **Divi → Theme Builder** and add a new template for posts in the Sermons category. Design it once:

- The **video** at the top.
- The **title**, **date**, and **series** below it, using **dynamic content** so Divi fills them in from each post automatically.
- The **summary** and scripture.
- A "More from this series" section at the bottom (more on that in step 3).
- A "Plan a Visit" call to action, saved as a global item from your Divi Library.

From now on, every sermon post uses this design. Your volunteers only fill in the blanks. They never touch the layout.

## Step 3: Build the archive page

Create a page called Sermons and add a **Blog** module set to the Sermons category, or, in Divi 5, use the **Loop Builder** to design exactly how each sermon card looks. Show the featured image (your series graphic), the title, and the date. Turn on pagination.

Then add a few quick filters at the top: buttons for your current series, your last series, and "Browse by book." Each one links to a tag page.

## Step 4: Make your events page clean itself up

This is my favorite trick. Divi's **condition options** let you show or hide any section based on the date and time.

Build your fall festival promo on the homepage, open its settings, and add a condition: display it only until the day after the festival. It disappears on its own. No more three-week-old promos. No more guests showing up for an event that already happened.

Do the same on your events page. Each event gets its own section with a date condition, and your page stays current even when you're on vacation.

You don't have to upload three years of old sermons. Start with this Sunday. Build the template, post one sermon, and go forward from there. Add older sermons in batches when you have a quiet week (if that ever happens).
