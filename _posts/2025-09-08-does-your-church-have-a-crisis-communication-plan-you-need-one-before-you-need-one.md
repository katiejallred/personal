Let me paint a scenario: It's a Tuesday morning. A screenshot surfaces on social media of something your lead pastor said three years ago. Within two hours, it's been shared 200 times. Your inbox is filling up. Your phone is ringing. Someone from your congregation is already posting about it publicly.

What do you do?

If you don't have a crisis communication plan, you're about to find out the hard way that making one in the middle of a crisis is a terrible idea.

## Every Church Needs a Plan

Churches are made up of imperfect people. Pastors say things they shouldn't. Staff members make mistakes. Social media posts go out before anyone reviews them. Accidents happen. Allegations surface. None of these things discriminate between large churches and small ones.

A crisis communication plan doesn't mean you expect the worst. It means you love your church enough to be prepared for it.

## The Core Elements of a Crisis Plan

### 1. Define the Goal

What is this plan trying to accomplish? Write it in one sentence. "The goal of this plan is to respond quickly, honestly, and in alignment with our mission when a crisis affects our church's reputation or community relationships."

### 2. Identify Your Audiences

Who needs to know what — and in what order? Your internal audience (staff, leadership board, congregation) should always know before the public does. In the absence of information, people make negative assumptions. Communicate early and often with your own people.

### 3. Designate a Spokesperson

The person with the most information is not always the best person to be on camera. Choose someone who represents the church well, stays calm under pressure, and can embody the tone you want to project. This doesn't have to be the lead pastor.

### 4. The 3 A's Framework

When your church is responsible for a mistake, leadership should:

- **Acknowledge:** Take responsibility. Don't be vague.
- **Apologize:** Ease the hurt of those affected. Mean it.
- **Act:** Make changes. Show the work.

Steps 1 and 2 can happen together. Step 3 sometimes needs a period of examination before you can speak to it publicly.

### 5. Social Media Protocol

Decide ahead of time how you'll handle social media in a crisis. Will you respond to every comment? Some? None publicly? Will you post a statement? Create a banner on your website? Whatever you decide, be consistent. Inconsistency in a crisis gives mixed signals and makes things worse.

## Build a Diverse Crisis Team

Your crisis team should not be a room full of people who all look alike, think alike, and have the same blind spots. Include laypeople from different ministry areas, people of different backgrounds, and people who will speak honestly about what they see.

Review your plan at least once a year. Update it when your church's situation changes. A plan that's never reviewed is a plan that won't work when you need it.

## The Goal Is Readiness

A crisis communication plan doesn't protect you from crisis — it protects your ability to respond to it well. That's the gift you give your team: clarity in an unclear moment, a road map when everything feels off the map.

Do the work now so you're not doing it then.
