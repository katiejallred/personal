It's Sunday morning, and the potluck sign-up sheet is taped to a folding table in the lobby. By the end of second service, it has three names, two coffee rings, and one entry that just says "casserole — Linda?" By Wednesday, nobody can find it.

Sound familiar?

Now multiply that by nursery shifts, greeters, the VBS snack rotation, the meal train for the family who just had a baby, and the carpool to youth camp. Somewhere in your church office, someone (maybe you) is chasing names on Saturday night and retyping them into Planning Center on Monday morning.

Here's the hard truth: **the clipboard is not a system.** And I built SignUpChurch because I got tired of watching church communicators burn their best hours on it.

## Full disclosure: I built this

I want to be upfront. SignUpChurch is mine. I've spent years teaching church communications and helping churches untangle their comms and volunteer chaos, and the same problem came up over and over. Churches don't lack willing volunteers. They lack a simple, clear way to say yes.

So yes, I'm biased. But I built it to solve a problem I've seen in church after church, and I think it will help yours.

## Why sign-ups matter more than you think

A sign-up sheet looks like a small thing. It isn't.

Every sign-up is someone raising their hand to serve. It's a first step toward belonging. When that step is confusing — *Where's the sheet? Did I already sign up? What time was I supposed to be there?* — people quietly stop raising their hands.

Jesus told us "the harvest is abundant, but the workers are few" (Matt 9:37). We can't afford to lose the workers we have to a lost clipboard or a buried group text.

Ask yourself a few questions:

- How many places do your sign-ups live right now? Paper? Google Forms? A free tool covered in ads?
- Who retypes those names into your church software, and how long does it take?
- Who sends the reminders? And what happens when they're sick that week?

If the answers make you tired, keep reading.

## What SignUpChurch does

Simply put, SignUpChurch does three things.

**First, you build a sheet in minutes.** Potluck, nursery, meal train, serve team, RSVP, carpool. Add time slots and set how many people each slot needs. On Pro, the AI sheet builder can draft it for you.

**Second, you share one link.** Put it in the bulletin, the church app, a text, or an email. Print the QR code for the lobby screen, or embed the sheet on your website. Volunteers don't need an account or an app. They tap the link, pick a slot, and they're done. There are no ads on any plan, so your people never have to scroll past a mattress sale to serve in the nursery.

**Third, every name lands where it belongs.** Volunteers get an email confirmation. On paid plans, they get a reminder 48 hours before they serve. And on Pro, every sign-up syncs straight into Planning Center, Church Community Builder, or MinistryPlatform, with Breeze, Tithe.ly/Elvanto, and Realm coming soon.

No more copy-paste Mondays. No more Saturday-night chase.

## Let's walk one example through it

Say your church is planning a fall festival. You need people for setup, the chili cook-off, face painting, the trunk-or-treat line, and teardown.

**The old way:** A sheet goes in the lobby for two Sundays. Your children's director keeps a separate list in her notes app. Someone makes a Google Form for chili entries. The week of the festival, you're texting people to confirm, and you still have no idea who's on teardown.

**The SignUpChurch way:** You build one sheet with a slot for each role and a cap on each one — six for setup, ten chili entries, four face painters. You drop the link in Sunday's announcements and the QR code on the lobby screen. People sign up from their pew. Each one gets a confirmation right away and a reminder two days out. If you're on Pro, every volunteer is already in your church software when the festival is over, so you can thank them and invite them to serve again.

Same festival. A fraction of the work. And your volunteers had a clear, easy way to say yes.

## Start small

You don't have to move every sign-up your church has ever made this week. That's okay! Start with one.

Pick the next event on your calendar — the potluck, the meal train, the Christmas Eve greeter team — and build that sheet on SignUpChurch. Share the link. Watch the names come in. Then decide what's next.

Be proactive instead of reactive. Automate the temporary, so you can invest in the eternal: the people behind every name on that sheet.

My prayer for you, church communicator, is that the next time someone raises their hand to serve, it's the easiest yes they've ever given — and that you get your Monday mornings back.
