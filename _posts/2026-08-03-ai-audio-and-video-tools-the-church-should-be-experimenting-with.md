Text and images were just the beginning. Generative AI has moved firmly into audio and video — and what's available right now would have seemed like science fiction five years ago.

For churches, this opens some genuinely exciting doors. It also raises some important ethical questions — but more on that in a moment. First, let's talk about the tools.

## SUNO: AI Music Generation

SUNO lets you generate original songs — complete with lyrics, vocals, and musical arrangements — from a simple text prompt. You specify the mood, genre, tempo, and theme, and SUNO composes something original.

For churches, the applications are still emerging, but imagine: a custom background instrumental for your podcast intro, a simple jingle for a children's ministry video, or an original worship-adjacent piece for a creative service element. This isn't replacing your worship team — but it's a remarkable tool for content creation at the margins.

Free tier available with limited generations per day.

## ElevenLabs: AI Voice Generation

ElevenLabs generates highly realistic AI voices from text — and yes, it can clone existing voices. You type a script, choose or create a voice, and get natural, expressive audio out the other side.

Practical church uses include narration for videos, voiceovers for church explainer content, accessibility tools for members with visual impairments, and multilingual translation of content. Imagine being able to publish your Sunday sermon in Spanish for your growing Spanish-speaking congregation without hiring a translator for every recording.

## HeyGen: AI Video and Avatars

HeyGen generates professional-quality videos using AI avatars. You provide a script, choose an avatar (or create one based on your own image), and HeyGen produces a video of the avatar delivering that script — lip-synced, natural, and without a camera or studio.

For churches, this could mean training videos, welcome messages for new members, or explainer content for ministry programs — produced at a fraction of the usual cost and time.

## The Ethical Dimension: Handle With Care

I'd be doing you a disservice if I didn't name this clearly: voice cloning and AI avatars carry real risks. Deepfakes, identity theft, the spread of misinformation — these are legitimate concerns with legitimate consequences.

The same technology that allows a church to translate its pastor's voice into five languages can be misused to put words in someone's mouth they never said. This is why the church needs to engage with these tools thoughtfully, not naively.

Guidelines I'd suggest: always disclose when AI-generated voices or avatars are used. Never use voice cloning to represent a real person without their explicit consent. Have a policy — before you need one.

The tools are powerful. Use them with the same ethical care you bring to everything else in ministry.
