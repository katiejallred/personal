I know what you're thinking. Everyone's on social media. Instagram Reels are getting thousands of views. Your pastor just went viral on TikTok. Why would we spend time worrying about a website?

Here's the thing: social media is a conversation starter. Your website is where the conversation gets real.

When someone new moves to town and starts looking for a church — and they will look online first, every single time — they're not going to follow you on Instagram before they show up on Sunday. They're going to Google "churches near me," click through to a few websites, and make a decision based almost entirely on what they find there. Your website is your first impression, your front door, your bulletin, and your guest services team all rolled into one page.

## What visitors are actually looking for

When a first-time visitor lands on your church's website, they have one question: "Is this a place for me?" Everything else — the service times, the address, the kids' ministry page — is just helping them answer that question.

Here's what they're scanning for in the first 10 seconds:

- **What kind of church is this?** Traditional? Contemporary? Charismatic? They're trying to locate themselves.
- **Do people like me go here?** Photos of real people matter enormously. Stock photos feel dishonest.
- **When do you meet and where?** Service times and address need to be impossible to miss.
- **What about my kids?** Parents with young children will navigate to the children's ministry page within seconds.

If your website doesn't answer these questions quickly and clearly, visitors will leave before they ever step through your doors.

## The three pages that matter most

You don't need a 40-page website. You need a few pages that work really hard. The three pages that have the highest impact for guest outreach are your homepage, your "I'm New" page, and your service times page.

Your **homepage** should feel like a warm handshake. It sets the tone, communicates your values, and gives people a clear next step. What do you want first-time visitors to do? Watch a message? Plan a visit? Make it obvious.

Your **"I'm New" or "Plan a Visit" page** is your most important page for outreach. This is where someone who's genuinely considering visiting goes to decide. Give them everything: what to expect, where to park, what to wear, what happens with their kids. Remove every possible anxiety before they arrive.

Your **service times page** sounds simple, but you'd be amazed how many churches bury this information. Put it everywhere. Header, footer, homepage, and its own dedicated page. When someone is on the fence, they should never have to hunt for when you meet.

## The mobile reality

More than 60% of your website visitors are on a phone. If your website isn't beautiful and functional on mobile, it doesn't matter how great it looks on a desktop. Test your site on your phone right now. Is the text readable? Do buttons work? Can you find service times within five seconds?

Google also uses mobile-first indexing, which means a poor mobile experience directly hurts your search ranking. If you're not showing up when people search for churches in your area, a slow or broken mobile experience might be why.

## One small thing you can do this week

Open your church website on your phone as if you've never seen it before. Pretend you just moved to town and you're looking for a church. Can you find service times in under 10 seconds? Do you get a sense of what this community is like? Is there a clear next step?

Write down what confused you or felt incomplete. That's your starting point.

Your website doesn't have to be perfect. It just has to be clear, honest, and easy to navigate. That's enough to open the door for someone who's looking for exactly what you have to offer.
