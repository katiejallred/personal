Let me say something that might ruffle a few feathers: your church already has a brand. You didn't choose it. You may not even like it. But it exists, in the minds of everyone who's ever driven past your building, visited your website, or heard your name mentioned at a backyard barbecue.

The question isn't whether your church has a brand. The question is whether you're intentional about it.

## Branding Is More Than a Logo

This is the most common misunderstanding I run into. A logo is part of your brand, but it's only one piece. Your brand includes your name, your tagline, the colors on your bulletin, the way your staff answers the phone, the tone of your social media, the state of your parking lot, and the first thing visitors feel when they walk through your doors.

Branding, at its core, is the total experience someone has with your church. It's what people say about you when you're not in the room.

## Jesus Was a Master Storyteller

The best brands are built on stories. Think Apple and Steve Jobs. Think Chick-fil-A and the cows. People remember stories.

Jesus understood this better than anyone. When asked a hard question, he rarely gave a direct answer. He told a story. The Good Samaritan. The Prodigal Son. The Lost Sheep. Stories that stuck, spread, and changed lives thousands of years later.

Your church has a story too. The question is: are you telling it consistently and compellingly?

## A Brand Audit: 5 Questions to Ask Right Now

Before you redesign your logo or refresh your website, ask these questions:

1. **What story do you want people to associate with your church?** And what story are they actually telling?
2. **Does your visual identity** (logo, colors, fonts) match how you want people to feel?
3. **Is your messaging consistent** across your website, social media, bulletin, and Sunday announcements?
4. **Do your physical spaces** (building, landscaping, signage) communicate the warmth and welcome you intend?
5. **What does your church name communicate** to someone who's never heard of you?

## Consistency Is the Secret

According to research, people need repeated, connected exposure for something to move from short-term to long-term memory. Your brand can't be a different color on the website than it is on the bulletin. Your tagline can't change every year. Your Instagram can't have a completely different tone than your Sunday program.

Consistency isn't boring. It's trust-building. People return to brands they recognize and trust. That includes churches.

## You Are the Guardian of the Brand

As a church communicator, one of your most important responsibilities is protecting the brand. That means pushing back when the youth pastor wants to use a random clip art image in the newsletter. It means saying no to the email that's written in three different fonts. It means advocating for consistency even when no one else seems to care.

No one will care about your church's brand as much as you do. That's not a burden. That's a calling. Own it.
