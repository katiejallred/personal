Every church communications director has had this moment: it's Thursday afternoon, you haven't posted since Tuesday, someone just texted you asking why the event wasn't on Instagram yet, and you're staring at a blank Canva document with absolutely no idea what to post.

A content calendar won't make social media easy. But it will make it survivable — and a lot more effective.

Here's how to build one that actually works for a church context, without needing a full-time social media team or a marketing degree.

## Start with your content pillars

Before you plan a single post, you need to decide what your church actually talks about online. Not everything — just the three to five topics that represent your community's values and the people you're trying to reach.

For most churches, content pillars look something like this:

- **Faith content** — Scripture, devotional thoughts, sermon quotes
- **Community** — Photos of real people, behind-the-scenes moments, volunteer spotlights
- **Practical help** — Tips for faith, family, mental health, relationships
- **Announcements** — Events, service times, what's coming up
- **Local love** — Celebrating your city, community partnerships, outreach moments

Once you have your pillars, you can map them to days of the week. Monday might always be a faith post. Wednesday might be community. Friday might be an announcement. Now you have a rhythm, and a rhythm is the backbone of a calendar.

## Build in batches, not day by day

The biggest mistake church communicators make is trying to create content day by day. That's exhausting and reactive. Instead, block two to three hours once a month to plan and create an entire month's worth of content at once.

Grab your church calendar, your content pillars, and your platform of choice, and map everything out. What events are coming up? What series is the pastor preaching? Are there any holidays or cultural moments worth acknowledging?

Then write the captions, create the graphics, and schedule everything. Yes, you'll need to leave room for spontaneous moments — a baptism, a surprise snowstorm that cancels service, a viral moment in the congregation. But having 80% of your content planned and scheduled means those spontaneous things feel like additions, not crises.

## The right posting frequency

There's no magic number, but here's what I've seen work for most church-sized accounts: three to five times per week on your primary platform, and less on secondary platforms.

Consistency matters more than frequency. Posting three times a week every week is dramatically better than posting ten times one week and going dark for two weeks. Algorithms reward consistency, and so do the real human beings following your account.

## A simple monthly planning template

Here's the template I use with churches I consult with. At the start of each month, fill in this grid:

- **Week 1:** What's the sermon series? What events are happening? What's the community moment worth capturing?
- **Week 2:** Same questions, plus — is there a volunteer or member story to tell this week?
- **Week 3:** Mid-month check. What needs a promotional push? What content performed well in Week 1 and 2 that you can build on?
- **Week 4:** What's coming next month? Start teasing it. What's the big story of this month you want to wrap up or celebrate?

## What to do when you fall behind

You will fall behind. Life happens. Somebody quits. The pastor changes the sermon series two days before Sunday. The event gets rained out.

When you fall behind, don't try to catch up. Just restart from today. Post something simple and genuine — even a phone photo of your team setting up for Sunday with a caption about what you're looking forward to. Then use the next quiet moment to rebuild your calendar.

Perfectionism will kill your social media strategy faster than anything else. Done is better than perfect, especially when you're one person trying to communicate for an entire church.
