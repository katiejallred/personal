I talk to a lot of church communicators who are exhausted. They're posting on Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, and Pinterest, and doing all of it poorly because they're spread so thin they can't keep up with any of it.

Here's the permission you've been waiting for: your church does not need to be on every social media platform.

## Why This Matters

In 2020, an estimated 3.81 billion people used social media globally. That number has only grown. Social media is not going anywhere, and it is not optional for the church. But the goal isn't to be everywhere. The goal is to be where your people are, and to show up there consistently and well.

A church with a thriving, engaged Instagram presence will do more gospel work than a church with neglected accounts on six platforms.

## Pick the Right Platforms for Your Audience

According to Pew Research, the three most used platforms among American adults are YouTube (81%), Facebook (69%), and Instagram (40%). That's a good starting point for most churches. But your specific congregation might skew differently. Older congregations lean Facebook; younger ones lean Instagram and TikTok.

Ask your people where they actually spend time. Then go there.

## Social Media Is a Conversation, Not a Billboard

This is where so many churches get it wrong. They treat social media like a digital bulletin, just announcing events and sermon series. But social media is interactive. You can have a real conversation. You can ask questions. You can respond to comments. You can build relationships that move from online to offline.

You can't talk to a billboard. You can talk to millions of people through social media. That changes everything about how you should approach it.

## Platform-Specific Tips

### Facebook

Still the most used platform for adults 35+. Great for event promotion, live video, and Facebook Groups. If your congregation skews older or suburban, Facebook should be your anchor platform.

### Instagram

Visual-first platform. Great for behind-the-scenes content, quote graphics, and reaching 25–40 year olds. Reels are essential right now for organic reach.

### YouTube

The highest-reach platform period, and it doubles as a search engine. If your church isn't uploading sermons to YouTube, you're leaving significant reach on the table. YouTube is where people search for answers to spiritual questions.

### TikTok

High reach, young audience. If you're trying to reach Gen Z, this is where they are. Short-form, authentic video works here, not polished productions.

## Do Less, Better

Pick two or three platforms. Make a content plan for them. Show up consistently. Respond to comments. Measure what's working. Improve.

Sustainability beats ambition every time in church communications. A post schedule you can maintain beats a grand plan you abandon in March.

The harvest is online. You just need to show up consistently enough to bring it in.
