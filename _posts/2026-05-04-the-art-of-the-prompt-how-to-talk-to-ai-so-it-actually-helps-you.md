Think of AI like a new intern. A brilliant, infinitely patient, never-complaining intern who has read more books than any human alive. But here's the catch: this intern has no context about your church, your voice, your situation, or what you actually need. They will do exactly what you ask — no more, no less.

If you ask for a pizza without specifying what kind, you might get anchovies. If you ask AI for "a sermon intro" without context, you might get something generic that sounds like it was written for a church that isn't yours. The quality of what you get out is directly tied to the quality of what you put in.

That input is called a prompt. And learning to write good ones is the single most valuable AI skill you can develop.

## The Anatomy of a Great Prompt

A strong prompt typically has four components:

1. **Role:** Tell AI who it should be. "You are a church communications director with 10 years of experience writing for evangelical audiences."
2. **Task:** Be specific about what you want. Not "write something about Easter" but "write a 200-word email inviting first-time visitors back for Easter Sunday."
3. **Context:** Give it the details it needs. Your church's name, tone, denomination, the audience, the goal.
4. **Format:** Tell it how you want the output. "Use short paragraphs. No jargon. End with a clear call to action."

## Persona-Based Prompting

Assigning a specific persona to the AI dramatically improves the output. Compare these two prompts:

**Weak:** "Write a social media post about our food pantry."

**Strong:** "You are a warm, conversational church communications director. Write three Instagram caption options for a post about our weekly food pantry. Our church serves about 200 families a week. Tone: hopeful and community-focused, not preachy. Include a gentle call to volunteer."

The second prompt gives AI everything it needs to be useful.

## Iterative Prompting: Have a Conversation

AI works best as a conversation, not a single transaction. Start broad, then refine. If the first draft isn't quite right, tell it:

- "Make it shorter."
- "That's too formal — sound more like a friend texting."
- "Add a Bible verse that fits naturally."
- "Give me three different versions with different tones."

## MetaPrompting: Let AI Help You Prompt

Here's a technique most people don't know: ask AI what it needs from you before you give it a task. Try this:

"I want to write a sermon series on forgiveness for a congregation dealing with a lot of community grief. What information do you need from me to help write the best possible series outline?"

AI will ask you clarifying questions. Your answers will make the final product dramatically better. This is called metaprompting — and it's one of the most powerful techniques available.

## Practice This Week

Take something you're already writing — a newsletter, an announcement, a social post — and write a prompt for it using all four components. Run it through ChatGPT or Claude. See what comes back. Refine it once or twice. You'll be surprised how quickly this becomes second nature.
