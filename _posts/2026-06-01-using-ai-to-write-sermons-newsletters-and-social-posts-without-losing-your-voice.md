Let me address the concern I hear most often before we go any further: "If I use AI to write my communications, won't they sound... robotic? Generic? Not like me?"

Yes — if you just copy and paste the first draft and hit send. No — if you use it the way it's actually meant to be used: as a starting point, a thinking partner, a first draft machine that removes the blank page and leaves the voice work to you.

Here's how to use AI for writing in a way that enhances your voice rather than erasing it.

## Sermon Preparation

AI is not going to preach your sermon. But it can help you prepare for it in ways that used to take hours.

- **Brainstorm sermon illustrations** — "Give me five modern, everyday analogies for the concept of grace in Luke 15." Pick the one that resonates and make it your own.
- **Explore theological angles** — "Summarize how three different theologians (one Reformed, one Catholic, one Wesleyan) interpret this passage." Get a broader view faster.
- **Build a sermon outline** — "Give me a three-point sermon outline for John 11 (Lazarus) for a congregation experiencing loss." Use it as a scaffold, not a script.

Your prayer life, your pastoral knowledge, your relationship with your congregation — none of that is replaceable. AI just handles the research and structure so you have more time for those things.

## Newsletters and Emails

For routine communications — weekly updates, event announcements, follow-up emails — AI is a game-changer. Give it the key information and let it draft. Then read it, cut what doesn't sound like you, add your actual anecdotes and pastoral warmth, and send.

A tip: feed AI a few examples of your past writing first. "Here are two emails I've written before. Match this tone and style for a new email announcing our summer sermon series." It learns your patterns quickly.

## Social Media

AI is genuinely excellent at social media captions. Give it the key message, the platform, the tone, and the goal, and ask for five variations. You'll probably find one that's close to perfect, or you'll mix elements from a few of them. Either way, you've gone from 20 minutes of staring at a screen to 2 minutes of editing.

Try: "Write 5 Instagram captions for a photo of our community garden workday. Tone: warm and community-focused. Goal: make people want to come next time. Include one question to drive comments."

## The Rule I Follow

I never post AI content without reading it out loud first. If I wouldn't say it in conversation, I don't publish it. That's the filter. AI drafts — you decide what's worth sharing.

Your voice is irreplaceable. AI is just the first draft. You're the editor. That's a good division of labor.
