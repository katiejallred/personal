I'm going to say something that might surprise you: Facebook Groups might be the most underused tool in the church communicator's toolkit.

Not the church's main Page. Everyone has one of those. I'm talking about Groups. The places where actual community happens, conversations start, and relationships deepen between Sundays.

## Why Groups Work

Facebook's algorithm heavily favors Group content over Page content. Posts from a Group show up in members' feeds more consistently than posts from a Page they've liked. This means your content is actually more likely to be seen in a Group than on your main church Page.

But the real power isn't reach. It's depth. Groups create a container for community. People come back because they feel like they belong, not because an algorithm served them your post.

## What Kind of Groups Should Your Church Run?

Almost anything works, but here are the ones I see working best for churches:

- **Small group private Groups**: A dedicated Group for each small group makes communication between members seamless. Share prayer requests, discussion notes, event invites, all in one place.
- **Ministry-specific Groups**: A Group for the choir, the women's ministry, the youth parents, the men's group. These stay active because the content is relevant and the group is small enough to be personal.
- **Community interest Groups** — This one is underrated for outreach. Create a Group around something your community cares about — hiking, local parenting, neighborhood news — and invite people in. Build relationships. Then move them offline.

## From Online to Offline

Here's the strategy I love most: create a community around a shared interest, build relationships in the Group, then use a Facebook Event to bring people together in person.

Say you create a hiking group for people in your area. You post trail recommendations, photos, tips. People connect. Then you create an event: a group hike. People show up. Life change happens in the context of real relationships — but in 2025, many of those relationships start online.

I did exactly this with an Easter event at a church I worked at. Used Facebook Events shared within Groups to reach several thousand people without spending a single dollar on ads.

## How to Keep a Group Active

The number one reason Groups die: no one posts in them consistently. You need a posting rhythm, and it doesn't have to be complicated.

Simple weekly prompts work beautifully:

- **Monday:** "Prayer request Monday — what can we pray for you about this week?"
- **Wednesday:** A midweek devotional thought or encouraging quote
- **Friday:** "What are you looking forward to this weekend?"

Consistent, low-effort prompts keep the Group alive and give members a reason to come back. You can even use a scheduler like SocialBee or RecurPost to automate evergreen prompts.

## Community Is Part of the Mission

We know from research that loneliness is one of the defining crises of our generation. The church has always been the answer to loneliness — a body, a family, a community of belonging. Facebook Groups are just one more way to extend that community beyond Sunday morning.

Use them. Your people need them.
