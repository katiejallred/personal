As this year winds down, I want to say something to every person who spends their days writing church emails no one reads, fixing graphics at 11 PM, managing the social media comments section, and holding the communication thread of a dozen ministries together with nothing but caffeine and grace:

What you do is ministry. Don't let anyone — or the endless to-do list — convince you otherwise.

## The Message Has Not Changed

Twenty years ago, the primary communicator of the church was the pastor — from the pulpit, through a printed bulletin, maybe a local radio spot. Today there are dozens of channels: websites, social media, email, podcasts, YouTube, text messages, digital ads, and whatever platform launches next year.

The medium has multiplied. The message has not changed. Hope. Salvation. Community. Jesus. That is what we are communicating, in every format, across every channel.

We are storytellers on a mission. The tools look different than they did for Paul, or for Luther, or for Billy Graham — but the mission is the same: to spread the gospel of Jesus to the ends of the earth using whatever means necessary.

## Communication Is How the Church Serves Its People

When a grieving widow can find the church's grief support group because someone made sure it was easy to find on the website — that's ministry.

When a young couple moves to town and finds your church through a well-targeted Instagram ad — that's ministry.

When a first-time visitor shows up on Sunday and already feels like they know what to expect because your website answered every question they had — that's ministry.

When you communicate clearly, everyone wins. The congregation knows what's happening and when. The community knows you exist and what you stand for. The leadership can focus on vision instead of logistics. And the people who need what your church offers can actually find it.

## A Prayer for Church Communicators

I want to close with something I mean from the bottom of my heart.

My prayer for you, church communicator, is that even when you are unseen, you know your value. That when there is chaos in a crisis, God is clear and words come easily. That you find a place where your ministry is respected and you are able to grow. And that you communicate the gospel clearly — until the whole world hears.

The church needs you. Keep going.
