## Reason 2: Clients can actually edit it

The fastest way to lose a client is to hand them a website they're afraid to touch. With Divi, they click on the words on the real page and type. That's it.

And you stay in control. Use the **Role Editor** to lock layouts and styles for the client's user role, so they can update content without breaking your design. Fewer emergency calls, happier clients.

## Reason 3: Your design system travels with you

This is where Divi 5 really earns its place for freelancers. Build your system once, then reuse it on every project:

- **Design variables** hold the client's colors, fonts, and sizes in one place.
- **Presets** save your styles for buttons, headings, and cards.
- **The Theme Builder** handles headers, footers, and templates.
- **Divi Cloud** (with Pro) stores your layouts, sections, and presets so you can pull them into any client site.

{% include screenshot.html src="/assets/uploads/2026/divi/builder-presets.jpg" alt="Divi 5's Preset Manager listing module types" caption="Presets turn your design decisions into a system you can reuse across projects." %}

When the client rebrands next year, you change a handful of variables instead of hunting through fifty pages. That's a billable hour saved, or a happier client, or both.
