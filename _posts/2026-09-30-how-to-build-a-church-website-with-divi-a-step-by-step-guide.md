## Step 1: Start with why, not with a template

Before you open Divi, answer one question: who is this website for?

It's not for your members. They already know when you meet. Your website is for the person who just moved to town, Googled "churches near me," and is quietly wondering, "Is this a place for me?" That person is the hero of your website. Your church is the guide.

Write down three things that person needs to know in the first ten seconds. For most churches, it's what kind of church you are, when and where you meet, and what happens with their kids. Keep that list next to you the whole time you build.

## Step 4: Set your brand once, use it everywhere

This is the step most churches skip, and it's why so many church websites look like five different people built them.

In Divi 5, set your colors, fonts, and sizes as **design variables** before you build another page. Then save your button and heading styles as **presets**. Every new page inherits them.

{% include screenshot.html src="/assets/uploads/2026/divi/builder-variables.jpg" alt="Divi 5's Variable Manager panel with groups for fonts, images, links, numbers, and text" caption="The Variable Manager. Set your church's colors and fonts here once, and every page uses them." %}

Simply put: decide once, and you'll never have to decide again. Your future volunteers will thank you.

## Step 5: Build the three pages that matter most

You don't need a 40-page website. You need three pages that work really hard.

**Your homepage.** It should feel like a warm handshake. Put a real photo of your people at the top, one clear sentence about who you are, your service times, and one big button: "Plan a Visit."

**Your Plan a Visit page.** This is your most important outreach page. Use Divi's accordion or toggle modules to answer the questions guests are too nervous to ask: Where do I park? What should I wear? What happens with my kids? How long is the service? Add a simple contact form so someone can tell you they're coming.

**Your service times and location page.** Add a map module, your address, and your times in big type. Then put your service times in your header or footer too, so they appear on every page.

## Step 6: Build templates for the pages you'll repeat

Divi's Theme Builder lets you design a template once and apply it everywhere. This is where Divi saves a church hours every month.

Build one template for your header and footer. Build one for sermon or blog posts. Build one for event pages. From then on, adding a new sermon or event is just filling in the blanks. The design takes care of itself.

## Step 7: Check it on your phone

Most of your visitors will see your website on a phone first. Divi lets you preview and adjust every section for desktop, tablet, and phone. Click the phone icon at the top of the builder and look at every page.

{% include screenshot.html src="/assets/uploads/2026/divi/builder-phone-view.jpg" alt="The Divi builder in phone view, showing the page at mobile width" caption="Phone view in the builder. Adjust font sizes and spacing for phones without changing the desktop design." %}

Then open your new site on your own phone and pretend you've never been to your church. Can you find service times in under ten seconds? Can you tap the address and get directions? If not, fix that before you launch.

## Step 8: Launch, then keep it alive

A website isn't a project. It's a ministry. Put a monthly reminder on your calendar to update your homepage, remove past events, and add new photos.

Then train one or two volunteers. Because Divi is visual, a volunteer can learn to update an event or swap a photo in an afternoon. That's how a team of one becomes a team of three.
