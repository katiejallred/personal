## Make your client the hero

Here's where most consultant websites go wrong: they're all about the consultant. My background. My certifications. My methodology.

Flip it. Your client is the hero of the story. You're the guide who helps them win. Your homepage should answer three questions, in this order:

1. **Who do you help?** Be specific. "Executive directors of small nonprofits" beats "organizations."
2. **What changes for them?** The result, in their words.
3. **What's the next step?** One clear button.

Your background matters, but it belongs on the about page, as proof you can deliver.

## Build the pages that book calls

**Work With Me.** List your offers plainly: what it is, who it's for, what they get, and how to start. Use Divi's pricing table module if you publish prices, or blurbs if you don't.

**About.** Your story, told as proof. Why you do this work, who you've done it for, and what you believe. Add a real photo.

**Speaking page (if you speak).** Event planners want to decide fast. Give them your topics, a short video clip, your bio in two lengths, your headshot to download, and past events. Use Divi's video module and tabs module to keep it tidy.

**Free resource.** Not everyone is ready to book. Offer a checklist, guide, or mini-course in exchange for an email address. Every Elegant Themes membership includes **Bloom** for opt-in forms, so you don't need another subscription.

**Book a call.** Embed your booking tool (I use TidyCal; Calendly works too) with a Divi code module, and point every "Book a Call" button on the site to this page.

## Proof beats promises

Anyone can say they're great at what they do. Show it instead.

- **Testimonials.** Use Divi's testimonial module with a name, title, and photo (with permission).
- **Logos.** If you've worked with organizations people recognize, add a simple logo row.
- **Results.** Specific, true outcomes. Never inflate a number.

Put proof on every page, not just a testimonials page. It belongs right next to every button.
