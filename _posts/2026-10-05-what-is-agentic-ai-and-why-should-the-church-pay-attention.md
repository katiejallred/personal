You've gotten comfortable with ChatGPT. You've tried generating some images. Maybe you've even gotten good at writing prompts. And now there's a new term floating around: agentic AI. What is it, and do you need to care about it yet?

Short answer: you don't need to use it today. But you need to understand what's coming.

## How Generative AI Works (The Baseline)

The AI most of us use today is reactive. You type a prompt, it responds. You ask a question, it answers. The conversation ends when you close the tab. Every time you open a new chat, you're starting from scratch. It's powerful — but it's still fundamentally a back-and-forth tool that requires a human to drive it.

## What Makes Agentic AI Different

Agentic AI doesn't wait for your next prompt. You give it a goal — not a single task, but an objective — and it figures out the steps, executes them, monitors the results, and adjusts along the way. It can interact with other systems, browse the web, send emails, update databases, and complete multi-step workflows largely on its own.

Think of the difference between hiring someone to write one email versus hiring a virtual assistant who manages your whole inbox, schedules meetings, drafts responses, and flags what needs your personal attention — without you having to direct every step.

## What This Could Mean for Churches

The possibilities are genuinely significant for ministry administration:

- An AI agent that monitors new visitor information, sends a follow-up email, schedules a pastoral call, and adds them to the right small group list — without anyone manually managing the process
- An agent that tracks prayer requests submitted online, organizes them by category, and prepares a weekly summary for the prayer team
- An agent that monitors your church's social media mentions, flags anything that needs a response, and drafts suggested replies for your review

## What to Watch For

Agentic AI is still in its early stages, but it's developing quickly. Here's how to stay ready:

- **Build good processes first.** An AI agent can only automate what's already well-defined. If your follow-up process is a mess, an AI agent will automate the mess. Get your systems clean before you automate them.
- **Maintain human oversight.** Agentic AI works best with a human in the loop for decisions that require pastoral judgment, sensitivity, or accountability.
- **Stay curious.** Watch what tools like OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic are releasing. The pace of change is fast. Churches that stay engaged will be better positioned than those who wait.

The era of AI that merely responds is giving way to AI that acts. The church should understand what that means — and be thoughtful about when and how we welcome it into ministry.
