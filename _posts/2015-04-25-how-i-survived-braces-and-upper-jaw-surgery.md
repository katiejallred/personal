## If you're facing jaw surgery

If you landed here because an orthodontist just said the words "jaw surgery," take a breath. It's a lot to take in. I'm not a doctor, and nothing here is medical advice. Your surgeon and orthodontist know your case, and they are the ones to trust with the details. But here's the general lay of the land and the questions I'd recommend bringing to your appointments.

## How braces and jaw surgery usually work together

Upper jaw surgery is a type of corrective jaw surgery (orthognathic surgery). An oral and maxillofacial surgeon moves the upper jaw into a better position when your bite is off in a way braces can't fix on their own.

It's usually a team effort. Braces typically come first to line up your teeth for where your jaw will be after surgery. Then comes surgery. Then braces usually stay on for a while afterward to finish the bite. That's why the whole process takes much longer than the surgery itself, and why, like me, you may find your timeline stretching.

## Questions to ask your surgeon and orthodontist

- Why do I need surgery, and what happens if I don't have it?
- Is this upper jaw only, or both jaws?
- How long will I be in braces before and after surgery?
- How long will I be in the hospital, and how much time should I plan off work or school?
- What will I be able to eat, and for how long?
- What are the risks, including numbness, and how often does feeling come back?
- How will pain and swelling be managed?
- How many follow-up visits will I need?
- Will my insurance cover this, and do you need pre-authorization? Is it billed as medical or dental?

Ask about insurance early. Jaw surgery can be billed differently from regular orthodontic work, and getting answers in writing before surgery can save you a lot of stress later.

## What recovery commonly involves

Every case is different, and your surgeon's instructions come first. That said, here's what many people deal with:

- **Swelling.** It's usually significant at first and goes down gradually over weeks.
- **A liquid, then soft, diet.** Plan on smoothies, protein shakes, broth, yogurt, and mashed foods for a while. A good blender helps.
- **Numbness.** Your upper lip, gums, palate, or teeth may feel numb. For many people, feeling returns slowly over months. For some, it takes much longer. A year after my surgery, I still couldn't feel the roof of my mouth or my front two teeth.
- **Congestion.** Upper jaw surgery is close to your sinuses, so a stuffy nose is common early on.
- **Fatigue.** Healing takes energy. Give yourself permission to rest.

Call your surgeon right away if something doesn't seem right, like fever, bleeding that won't stop, trouble breathing, or pain that gets worse instead of better.

## Be patient with yourself

Recovery is a marathon, not a race. Stock your kitchen before surgery, line up someone to help for the first several days, and don't measure your progress day by day. Look at it month by month.

Braces and upper jaw surgery were hard. But God was faithful through every part of it, including the bills. If you're about to start this journey, He'll be faithful to you too.
