*Updated September 2026 with context on Ecclesiastes 7, related Scripture on grief, reflection questions, and what I'd tell you today.*

Before reading it would probably be best to read: [Ecclesiastes 7](http://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ecclesiastes+7&version=NIV)!

I try not to take anything too seriously. Life, even death, have become laughable subjects for me. After you go through so much what can you do? But I recently read this and it's changing my mind a bit:

> It is better to go to a house of mourning   
>        than to go to a house of feasting,   
>        for death is the destiny of every man;   
>        the living should take this to heart.   
>  **Sorrow is better than laughter,   
>        because a sad face is good for the heart.**   
>  The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning,   
>        but the heart of fools is in the house of pleasure. (Ecc. 7: 2-4)

Now I'm wondering if I'm going about this all wrong. Understand, I've never really mourned. I find times when I'm alone for too long that my mind dwells on the death of my loved one's and I can't help but cry for hours on end. It's been a year and a half and I still cry about my dad's death once a week, while putting on a front that it completely doesn't bother me at all. But I'm finding that:

> When times are good, be happy;   
>        but when times are bad, consider:   
>        God has made the one   
>        as well as the other.   
>        Therefore, a man cannot discover   
>        anything about his future. (Ecc. 7.14)

 This is so true. I wish I had some inquisitive things to say about all this, but at the moment that's what I got.

## What is Ecclesiastes 7 actually saying?

Ecclesiastes is written by "the Teacher," a wise man traditionally connected to Solomon, who looks hard at life "under the sun" and refuses to pretend it's simple. Chapter 7 is a string of "better than" proverbs. A good name is better than fine perfume. The day of death is better than the day of birth. Sorrow is better than laughter (Ecclesiastes 7:1-4).

That sounds gloomy until you see the point. The Teacher isn't saying joy is bad. Earlier in the same book he says there is a time to weep and a time to laugh (Ecclesiastes 3:4). He's saying the house of mourning teaches you something the party never will. Funerals make you remember that your days are numbered. That's the same prayer Moses prays when he asks God to teach us to number our days so we can gain a heart of wisdom (Psalm 90:12).

Verse 14 finishes the thought. Good days and hard days both come from God's hand, and you can't see around the corner to know which is next. So you don't have to fake the good or run from the hard.

## Grief is not a lack of faith

If you're like I was, laughing everything off so nobody sees you cry, the Bible gives you permission to stop. Jesus wept at the tomb of Lazarus, even knowing He was about to raise him (John 11:35). He said those who mourn are blessed because they will be comforted (Matthew 5:4). Paul tells believers they will grieve, just not like people who have no hope (1 Thessalonians 4:13). And the Lord is near to the brokenhearted (Psalm 34:18).

Mourning isn't the opposite of trusting God. Sometimes it's how you bring your pain to Him honestly instead of hiding it behind a front.

## Questions to sit with

- Where are you "putting on a front" right now, with other people or with God?
- What loss have you never really let yourself mourn?
- What would it look like to bring that grief to God this week instead of laughing it off?
- Who is one person you could be honest with?

## What I'd tell you today

Let yourself grieve. Laughter is a gift, and I still believe that, but it makes a poor hiding place. Find a friend, a pastor, a counselor, or a grief group at a local church and say the thing out loud. If your grief has turned into thoughts of harming yourself, please call or text 988 in the US right away.

I wrote more about this a few years later in [three lessons learned from grief](/three-lessons-learned-from-grief/).

Weep with those who weep (Romans 12:15), and let them weep with you. God has made the good days and the hard ones, and He is present in both.
